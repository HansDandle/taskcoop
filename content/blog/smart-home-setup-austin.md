Austin homes range from 1940s bungalows in Hyde Park to 2023 builds in Leander, and smart home technology works very differently across them. Here's a practical breakdown of what's actually worth installing, what needs a professional, and what to skip entirely.

## The Foundation: Get Wi-Fi Right First

Nothing else matters if your Wi-Fi is bad. Austin homes frequently have dead zones because:

- Many are large single-story ranch layouts where the router is at one end and the far bedrooms are 50-60 feet away
- Thick stucco and masonry exterior walls, common in Texas construction, absorb signal heavily
- The router ends up in a bad spot, stuck in a corner wherever the cable entry point happens to be

**What works:** A mesh network system (Eero, Google Nest WiFi, or TP-Link Deco). For most Austin homes under 2,500 sq ft, two nodes is enough. Larger homes or two-story layouts typically need three.

**What doesn't:** Powerline adapters in Austin homes are unreliable because the electrical wiring in many older central Austin neighborhoods is inconsistent. A mesh system with a wired backhaul is almost always better.

A task.coop Tech & Smart Home member can assess your layout, recommend the right system, and set it up correctly, including configuring separate IoT and guest networks if you want them.

## Smart Thermostats: Yes, Always

The ROI on a smart thermostat in Austin is faster than almost anywhere in the country. Summers are brutal and long. A Nest Learning Thermostat or Ecobee typically pays for itself in 6-12 months through reduced cooling costs.

**Installation note:** If your HVAC system uses a heat pump (common in Austin), not every smart thermostat is compatible. An Ecobee is generally the safer choice. A member can check your wiring before you buy.

## Smart Locks: Practical, Not Just Cool

For Austin homeowners who do short-term rentals, have regular housekeepers, or just lose keys constantly, a smart lock is genuinely useful. The Schlage Encode and Yale Assure work well. Avoid cheap Wi-Fi locks; they tend to fail in Austin's heat.

**Important:** Replacing a deadbolt requires proper door prep and alignment. A poorly installed smart lock is a security liability. This is one task worth having done properly.

## What to Skip (For Now)

- **Smart blinds.** Expensive, failure-prone, and the ROI doesn't work out for most Austin homes.
- **Whole-home audio.** Great if you're building new; retrofitting is messy and expensive.
- **Smart appliances.** The apps are generally worse than the physical controls.

## Video Doorbells and Security Cameras

Ring and Nest doorbells are straightforward DIY installs if your existing doorbell has power. If not, or if you want cameras at the roofline, eaves, or driveway, hire someone. Ladder work on Austin homes (many with steep rooflines) is not a casual weekend project.
